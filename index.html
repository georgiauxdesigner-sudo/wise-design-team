    Four rules you'll use every day

    If you remember nothing else from this guide, remember these.

    Rule 1

    Check all client materials available

    Check the client’s Google Drive, live website, Figma file, or ask WISE Claude. If none have details, work from the ClickUp task description.

    Rule 2

    Track every minute

    Always log your time on every task before you change its ClickUp status to Complete, even if it took a minute.

    Rule 3

    Submit with tags and links

    Tag your Design Lead and the PM on the parent task, with Drive, Figma and source file links.

    Rule 4

    @mention when it matters

    An @mention means "please look at this." If you're mentioned, react with an emoji so people know you saw it.

    Tracking your time

    Why

    Track every minute of work, even 1-minute tasks. Time data gives us proof of effort in tough client conversations, and it shows the team how much each person is carrying.

    Use this whenever you can.Preferred

    1. Go to the My Working Delivery view at the Everything level.
    2. Find your task and click the play button.
    3. Stop the timer when you finish, either from the task or from the global tracker.

    Forgot to start the timer? Add the time by hand.

    1. Open the task and click Add Time.
    2. Type the duration, for example 1 hour.
    3. Change the date and time to when you actually did the work. This is the step people miss.
    4. Add a short note, for example "Client meeting with Charles & Matt."

    Five minutes, every day.Required

    1. Open the Timesheets widget and pin it to your sidebar.
    2. Switch to the Time Entries view. It shows your day as time blocks.
    3. Look for gaps, timers you forgot to stop, and durations that look wrong.
    4. Click any start or end time to fix it.

    Chapter 5

    How every creative task runs

    Each task type has its own checklist (see the Task Library next), but they all follow the same four phases.

    Review

    Read the ClickUp task description and everything linked to it, plus the four client docs. Note any gaps or questions in Client Notes.

    Study

    Look at what already exists: the live site, the Figma file, past versions of the same deliverable.

    Create

    Design on-brand using the client's colors, fonts and tone, at the correct size and inside the safe zones.

    Check & submit

    Check your work against the instructions, then submit in ClickUp and tag the AM/PM and Tris.

    How work gets reviewed

    Seven task types have a full quality checklist (marked Quality checklist in the Task Library). Each one goes through two reviews before it reaches the client.

    Review 1 · You

    Self-review

    Tick every item on the checklist before you submit. Don't submit incomplete work.

    Review 2 · Tris

    Quality check before the client

    Tris, the Design Manager, checks the work against the checklist and can send it back to you as incomplete.

    Rule

    Quality standards are never skipped because of a deadline. If the work needs more time, the timeline gets adjusted instead.

    When changes are requested

    How much you re-check depends on how big the change is. Each task card has its own examples.

    MinorSmall swap or tweakExampleSwap one image, edit a headline, adjust a colorRe-checkSpot-check the parts you changed, plus export settings and ClickUp tags
    ModerateDesign or sourcing changeExampleLayout restructured, new concept, or re-sourcing for several pagesRe-checkGo through every item in the build or sourcing step for what's affected
    MajorBrief-level changeExampleNew brand or creative direction, full redesignRe-checkRun the full checklist on every affected deliverable. Most tasks also need Design Principles re-scoring
    Log it

    For every revision round, note the date, what changed, which checklist sections you re-checked, and who reviewed it, in the task's change log.

    Walkthrough: Source a blog image

    The most common task, step by step. Use it as a model for how the other tasks should go.

    Review order:You→Tris BerganioHow reviews work
    1

    Review the design materials

    Understand the brand's visual direction and how its current designs work before you start.

    2

    Get to know the current blog

    Your image should fit the blog's existing structure, tone and look.

    3

    Find images on Adobe Stock

    Pick high-quality visuals that match the brand and the post.

    Always

    Filter your Adobe Stock search to Standard License.

    4

    Check your work with the team

    Catch mistakes before anything goes to the PM.

    5

    Export, name and submit

    Set the file up so anyone can tell where it goes just from its name.

    1. Open the Website Blog Images file in Figma.
    2. Check the ⚠️ SAFE ZONE ⚠️ page for the correct blog image size and safe area.
    3. Find the page for your client.
    4. Name the file: Client Name_Blog Title. Try it below.

    DoSeparate the client and title with an underscore _Capitalize each word: GC PlumbingBe specific enough that the placement is obvious
    Don'tUse lowercase or hyphens: gc-plumbingUse generic names: image8, group53
    1. Use these export settings in Figma:
    Export
    1x ⌄JPEG ⌄
    Export [frame name]

    Scale 1x, format JPEG. Export the frame that's already set to the correct blog size.

    Chapter 9

    Walkthrough: Creative briefs

    A creative brief turns the client’s documents and interviews into one shared reference the whole team builds from. Since 2026, every brief uses the same standard format, with the content generated in the client’s Claude project.

    Review order:You→Tris BerganioHow reviews work

    How a brief comes together

    1. Client documents & interviews
    2. Information extraction & synthesis
    3. Brief structure & visual direction
    4. Shared reference for execution

    Helps the whole team work with clarity:

    DesignersTris & Josh
    CopywritersMessaging
    DevelopersImplementation
    Example

    Use the RISE Financial Solutions creative brief in Figma as your reference for the standard format.

    Step by step

    1

    Check the client’s Claude project files

    Claude can only use what’s in the project. Without the client’s files, the brief will be generic or wrong.

    2

    Generate the brief content in Claude

    Claude pulls the key information out of the project files and organizes it into the brief’s sections.

    3

    Paste it into the client’s Figma file

    The Figma file holds the brief’s structure and visual direction.

    4

    Review and share

    Once approved, the brief becomes the reference designers, copywriters and developers work from.
